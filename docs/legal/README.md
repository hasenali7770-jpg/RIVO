# Legal documents — drafts, not legal advice

These are **placeholders written by a developer, not a lawyer.** They describe
accurately what the RIVO system actually does — every data claim in them was
checked against the schema and the code — so an Iraqi lawyer reviewing them is
starting from a truthful description rather than a template. They must be
reviewed and adopted by counsel before RIVO publishes them or submits the app
to either store.

Every `[…]` marks something only RIVO can fill in: the registered company name,
the address, the contact addresses, and the governing-law clause.

| File | Purpose | Needed for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`PRIVACY_POLICY.md`](./PRIVACY_POLICY.md) | what is collected, why, how long | App Store and Play Store both require a public URL |
| [`TERMS_OF_USE.md`](./TERMS_OF_USE.md) | the agreement with users | store submission |
| [`LISTING_POLICY.md`](./LISTING_POLICY.md) | what may be listed and what gets a listing rejected | shown to sellers before their first listing |
| [`AI_DISCLOSURE.md`](./AI_DISCLOSURE.md) | what the photo enhancement does and does not do | shown at the enhancement step |
| [`COMMUNITY_POLICY.md`](./COMMUNITY_POLICY.md) | reporting road conditions and reporting listings | shown at first incident report |

Host the privacy policy and terms at stable public URLs before store
submission — both stores reject a build whose privacy policy URL 404s.
